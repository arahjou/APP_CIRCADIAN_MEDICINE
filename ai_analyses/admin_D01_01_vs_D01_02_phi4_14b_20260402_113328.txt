## Summary:
Your activity levels have decreased slightly, but your light exposure has increased significantly, which may be helping improve your sleep duration.

1) What looks off (in simple terms):
- Your overall activity level is a bit lower than before.
- You're getting more light during the day, which might affect your body's natural clock.
- The timing of when you fall asleep in the middle of the night has shifted.
- There are recommendations to reduce light exposure after waking up.

2) What looks good — keep doing this:
- Your daily activity and light patterns have become more consistent.
- You're getting more sleep, which is great for your health.
- Light management during sleep has improved, reducing unnecessary exposure.

3) Your 7-day action plan:
- **Wake Up Early:** Try to wake up at the same time every day around 7 AM (morning light helps set your body clock).
- **Get Outside:** Spend at least 30 minutes outside in natural daylight each afternoon.
- **Limit Evening Light:** Reduce screen time and bright lights after 8 PM to prepare for sleep.
- **Maintain a Sleep Schedule:** Go to bed and wake up at the same times every day, even on weekends.
- **Stay Active:** Aim for at least 30 minutes of physical activity during the day (like walking or stretching).
- **Create a Relaxing Bedtime Routine:** Engage in calming activities like reading or listening to soft music before bed.

4) How these patterns can affect you:
- **More Light Exposure → Better Sleep Timing → Reduced Risk of Cognitive Issues:** More daylight exposure helps your body clock align better, which may reduce the risk of cognitive decline (insomnia symptoms and sleep duration are linked to dementia risks; PMID: 37676841).
- **Consistent Activity Patterns → Stable Energy Levels → Improved Mood:** Regular activity can help keep your energy levels steady throughout the day, potentially improving mood.
- **Improved Sleep Duration → Better Restorative Processes → Enhanced Overall Health:** Getting enough sleep supports body repair and cognitive function.

Safety note:
If you notice significant changes in your sleep or daily routine that concern you, consider consulting a healthcare professional for personalized advice.

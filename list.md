## Metrics calculation

### The ones you listed

**1. CPD – Composite Phase Deviation**

* Quantifies the misalignment between an individual’s behavioral rhythms (e.g., sleep timing, eating timing) and their expected or “ideal” phase.
* Often computed as the deviation of daily sleep or activity midpoints from the individual’s average.
* Useful for measuring day-to-day irregularity or misalignment.

**2. SRI – Sleep Regularity Index**

* Captures how consistent sleep–wake patterns are across days.
* Values close to 100% mean highly regular timing of sleep/wake across days, while lower values mean irregularity.
* Sensitive to both delayed bedtimes and variable wake-up times.

**3. IS – Interdaily Stability**

* Derived from actigraphy.
* Quantifies how well an individual’s activity pattern aligns with the 24-h day (entrainment to light/dark cycle).
* Higher IS = stronger synchronization with external zeitgebers.

**4. L5 – Least Active 5 Hours**

* The 5-hour window with the lowest average activity within a 24-h cycle.
* Often used as a proxy for nighttime rest/sleep intensity.
* The **onset time of L5** can also be used as a phase marker.

**5. M10 – Most Active 10 Hours**

* The 10-hour window with the highest average activity in a day.
* Reflects the most active period (usually daytime).
* The **midpoint of M10** is often used as an estimate of activity phase.

**6. Cosinor Analysis**

* Fits a cosine function to rhythmic data to quantify:

  * **Mesor** (mean level),
  * **Amplitude** (strength of the rhythm),
  * **Acrophase** (timing of the peak).
* Widely used in circadian rhythm research for continuous variables (e.g., core body temp, melatonin, activity).

### Other important circadian metrics

* **IV – Intradaily Variability**

  * Measures rhythm fragmentation within the day (how often someone switches between rest and activity).
  * High IV = more fragmented rhythm (common in older adults or in disease states).

* **RA – Relative Amplitude**

  * Ratio between M10 and L5 values: $(M10 - L5) / (M10 + L5)$.
  * Reflects the strength of the rest-activity rhythm.

* **Social Jetlag**

  * Difference between sleep midpoint on workdays vs. free days.
  * Quantifies circadian misalignment due to social obligations.

* **Phase Angle of Entrainment**

  * The relationship between internal circadian markers (like dim-light melatonin onset, DLMO, or core body temperature minimum) and external cues (like sleep time or light exposure).

* **DLMO – Dim Light Melatonin Onset**

  * Gold-standard marker of circadian phase, measured via saliva/blood/urine.

* **Mid-Sleep on Free Days (MSF)**

  * From questionnaires (like the Munich ChronoType Questionnaire).
  * Used to estimate chronotype.

* **Intradaily Amplitude (IA)**

  * Strength of the circadian rhythm by comparing peak vs. trough activity within a day.
